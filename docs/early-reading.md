# Letters and Sounds teaching design

Reviewed 16 October 2026; revised for Sarah’s age (two) and parent-reported familiarity with M, S, A, P, and long E (/ee/ as in see). David confirmed that “hard E” means long E; short E (/ĕ/ in egg) is a separate correspondence and is not assumed familiar. These sections provide brief supported practice; app completion and two-choice recognition do not establish independent reading mastery.

The age-specific reference is NAEYC’s [Toddlers and Reading: Describe but Don’t Drill](https://www.naeyc.org/our-work/families/toddlers-and-reading-describe-don%27t-drill): prioritize responsive talk, shared books, and playful sound imitation. The app is a brief shared game, not a toddler reading program. The original phonics reference is the US Institute of Education Sciences / What Works Clearinghouse [Foundational Skills practice guide](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/WWC/PracticeGuide/21/Published), especially recommendations 2 and 3 (strong evidence): connect speech segments to letters; explicitly teach decoding and writing. Its [family activities](https://ies.ed.gov/use-work/resource-library/resource/other-resource/supporting-your-childs-reading-home) show how to link sounds, blend, and write at home. The guide also recommends daily connected-text reading. The exact interface, pocket size, activity count, and migration strategy below are product design decisions, not independently validated interventions.

## What changed and why

- Keep Letters' established phonics-first sequence. Sounds now introduces m, s, short a, t, p before the wider consonant inventory, making early blending possible. Do not make children learn 23 consonants before meeting a short vowel.
- Two sounds per visit, usually a focus sound with a familiar M/S/A/P anchor. Both are modeled before two matching turns. There are only five required tiny steps: two introductions, a tap/copy invitation, and two supported matches. No compulsory uppercase/lowercase conversion, sound-position quiz, spelling, or writing. Uppercase is prominent in Letters, with lowercase modeled alongside it. This is supported play, not delayed unaided assessment.
- Model pictures and keywords support meaning; sound-to-print questions use plain letters without spelling the answer in the question. Do not ask children to discriminate identical printed alternatives such as two th pronunciations or identical sounds such as c/k.
- Some two-sound pockets offer optional word play. An adult must tap “Listen together” before the word model starts; “All done” skips it with the same normal lesson reward. Audited pronunciation-matched words use the two modeled sounds. This is an invitation to hear sounds joining, not a decoding expectation for a two-year-old.
- Oral imitation is optional: “Want to say it with me?” advances with “Next,” without claiming she spoke. Use the picture as a starting point for a real conversation. No handwriting task or required finger-counting sequence.
- The opening examples are familiar moon, sun, apple, tree, and pig. Correct misleading short-vowel keywords (apple, egg, octopus) and x → fox. /ng/ in ring and /ks/ in fox are final sounds; /er/ in bird is medial. No generic “starting sound” question for these.
- Letters uses the existing IPA-controlled trimmed sound library, with k + s for x. Sounds uses these same recordings wherever its exact IPA is available; other Sounds items retain their existing packaged audio. No isolated spelling falls back to browser letter-name speech. Whole keywords may use whole-word browser speech if their recorded clip is unavailable. Existing legacy letter-keyword clips remain in the downloadable pack but are bypassed by Letters, since some contain the old examples.
- Feedback acknowledges practice, marks help after the first error, and avoids claiming that completing a two-choice lesson proves mastery. Both supported and unsupported completions retain normal rewards.

## Progress and boundaries

Keep profile IDs and letter resume indices. Sounds' reordered sequence uses an account-scoped `sarah-progress-sarah-phonemes-level-2:phonics-v2` key. On first visit, map the legacy sequence to the earliest unvisited sound in the new sequence; leave the legacy key intact. Ignore the old numerical Continue snapshot for Sounds. This can revisit familiar sounds while filling earlier gaps. Original Words and the 100 Easy Lessons sequence remain separate.

Aim for roughly 30–60 seconds of shared play, without a timer, speed requirement, or obligation to finish. This duration is a design target, not a research-prescribed dose. Stop earlier if the child is tired. Share books, talk about stories, and draw/write together beyond these app activities. Device recognition tasks cannot replace these interactions.

## Verification

`npm run verify:early-reading` checks every lesson for bounded pockets, no unmodeled answer choices, genuine distinct alternatives, two matching turns, absence of formal case/position tests, optional word play, pronunciation-matched decodability, corrected sound positions, and conservative progress migration. Also run build, lint, phonics audio validation, guided-word regression checks, and the deck/media/offline/account checks. The checks establish software/content invariants, not learning efficacy or an acoustic audit of every retained recording.
